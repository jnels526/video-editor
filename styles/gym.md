# Style: gym

> Learned on 2026-11-27 from 5 reference reels in `references/gym/`.
> The theme: **women doing hard things, plus tips to succeed.** The lift itself is the hook. Nobody talks to camera, music carries it, and short text gives the tip.
> All 5 are 720x1280, 30 fps, about -14 LUFS. Cut counts include jump cuts on a locked tripod, which were checked by eye.
> Claude adds your feedback to **Do / Don't** after each edit, so the style improves over time.

## What this style is for
- Type of content: one movement or a short routine, either as a tip ("do this to fix X") or as a strength moment (a heavy or impressive lift)
- Who is on screen: you alone, filmed from a tripod. No voice. Music or a trending sound is the only audio (ref: all 5).

## Format
- Aspect ratio / resolution: 9:16, 1080x1920
- Frame rate: 30 fps (ref: all 5)
- Typical length: 8-25 seconds for a single tip or lift (ref: Dani Ferreira 0:08, THERESA 0:13, NA 0:19, Vic El'Azar Fries 0:23). Use up to 40 seconds for a routine with several exercises (ref: Belinda 0:40).

## Hook (first 3 seconds)
- How the video opens: **frame 1 is already mid-rep.** No walk-up and no setup (ref: Dani Ferreira @ 0:00, NA @ 0:00, Vic El'Azar Fries @ 0:00 snatch with the bar overhead by 0:02.5).
- On-screen text in the hook: one promise sentence that says what the viewer gets, all lowercase, e.g. "do this ___ and never suffer from ___ again", "add in this movement to ___" (ref: THERESA @ 0:00, Dani Ferreira @ 0:00). Keep it on screen for the whole video. A routine uses a title instead (ref: Belinda @ 0:00 "My leg day / warmup routine").
  For a pure strength moment, use no text. The lift is the hook (ref: Vic El'Azar Fries).
- Cuts in the first 3 seconds: 0-2. If you cut, show a different angle of the same rep or a 0.4s close-up insert, e.g. hands on the handle or a knee on the bench (ref: Dani Ferreira @ 0:00.7, NA @ 0:02).

## Pacing
- Average shot length: 2-3 seconds for tips and routines (ref: NA 2.3s, Belinda about 3s). Hold 6-8 seconds on a single heavy lift so the whole rep is seen (ref: Vic El'Azar Fries, THERESA).
- Pauses: not relevant, because there is no speech
- Cut timing: **on the movement, not the music beat.** Cut at the end of a rep or when the exercise changes. None of the 5 references cut on the beat.
- Speed changes: none. Real speed only (ref: all 5).

## Captions and on-screen text
- Spoken-word captions: no (ref: 0 of 5)
- **Hook / promise line**: bold rounded sans (like the Instagram "Classic" or Montserrat SemiBold), off-white, about 44px, thin dark outline, all lowercase, 2-3 lines of about 5 words, centered 27-34% from the top. It stays on screen the whole video (ref: THERESA @ 0:00-0:13).
- **Exercise labels** (routines and variations): heavy white bold italic sans, Title Case, about 60px, soft drop shadow, centered about 80-85% from the top, 2-4 words, with reps on a smaller second line (about 32px) when useful, e.g. "Cossack Squat / 16 reps". Change the label exactly on the cut to the next exercise (ref: NA @ 0:04.4, 0:09.6, 0:14.6; Belinda @ 0:03).
  Alternative: the label sits mid-screen at 48-52% in small regular white (ref: Belinda).
- Routine title: heavy rounded sans in golden yellow (#F5B800), about 70px, soft shadow, centered at 46-50%, with a smaller yellow subtitle under it (about 30px). It disappears at the first cut (ref: Belinda @ 0:00-0:02).
- Colors: white text. Yellow only for a routine title. No boxes, no highlighted words.
- Animation: none. Text appears and disappears on cuts.
- All caps?: no, use lowercase for the promise line and Title Case for labels
- Emojis: none (ref: all 5)

## Footage and framing
- Main shots: tripod, full body, you alone. Use a side angle, a low angle from the floor looking up, or a 3/4 back angle (ref: Dani Ferreira, NA, Vic El'Azar Fries).
- Second angle: a closer shot (thighs to head) of the same move for variety (ref: NA @ 0:04, THERESA @ 0:05.3)
- Keep effort faces and real strain in (ref: NA). Don't cut away from the hardest part of the rep.
- Zooms / punch-ins: none in the references. If one angle runs over 6 seconds, a slow 5% digital punch-in is allowed.

## Graphics
- Titles / labels: only the text above. No lower thirds, logos or stickers.
- Tool to use: HyperFrames for the text overlays (static text, so no Remotion needed)

## Transitions
- Hard cuts only (ref: all 5)

## Color
- Overall look: keep it close to natural phone footage. Don't use a heavy grade. Lift exposure slightly if the gym is dark, and keep skin tones true (ref: all 5). The location gives the mood (bright white walls, moody window light, colorful mural), so pick a good spot to film.

## Music & sound effects
- Music type and energy: a trending sound or a confident hip-hop or pop track, 80-150 BPM (ref: all 5). Only use music from `assets/` that you're allowed to use, or add the trending sound in the Instagram app when posting.
- Music level: the music is the main audio. Mute the gym's own sound, or keep it very low under the music.
- Sound effects: none (ref: all 5)
- Final loudness: -14 LUFS (all 5 references measured -14.0 to -14.2)

## Ending
- End mid-rep or at the top of the last rep, with no outro and no call to action (ref: NA, THERESA). Where possible, end on the same angle as the opening so the video loops (ref: Dani Ferreira).
- Routines can end on a short lead-in line, e.g. "now let's lift heavy" (ref: Belinda @ 0:37.6).

## Do / Don't
*Your corrections, saved as rules. Never deleted, only added to or refined.*

**Do**
- Start on the hardest or most impressive moment of the movement
- Make the text a promise or a clear instruction, not a description

**Don't**
- Don't copy other creators' exact hook sentences, audio clips or names. Write your own promise line.
- Don't add speed ramps, transitions or emojis

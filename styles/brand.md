# Style: brand (faceless personal brand)

> Written on 2026-11-27 from Jas's brand brief and a 10-image moodboard of editorial magazines, film-photo collages and a video essay. These are still images, so there are no video references yet.
> Timing rules below are starting points. Refine them with `/learn-style brand <links>` once you find 3-5 video essays you like.
> **The idea:** your page is a small independent publication. You're its narrator, researcher and editorial voice. People follow for **your perspective**, not generic information.
> Claude adds your feedback to **Do / Don't** after each edit, so the style improves over time.

## What this style is for
- **Your own voice, no face.** The voiceover is always you: your real voice, Southern accent, humor and curiosity. **Never an AI voice.**
  Hands, feet, your back, your desk or your notebook may appear, but never your face (for now; showing it occasionally later won't break the brand).
- Tone: conversational, investigative, thoughtful, sometimes funny. Curious without being pretentious.
- Three formats:

| Format | Job | Built from | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mini-documentary** | Teach | Your voice over archival footage, stock video, statistics, maps and research | "Did we accidentally design loneliness into our cities?" |
| **Visual essay** | Opinion | Conversational voiceover with bold typography, relevant imagery and cultural references | "Not every hobby needs to be monetized." |
| **Visual diary** | Story | Your own footage of everyday life, filmed from your point of view, with personal narration | "I wanted a better social life, so I started building one." |

## Format
- Aspect ratio / resolution: 9:16, 1080x1920
- Frame rate: 24 fps for a film feel
- Typical length: 45-90 seconds. A visual diary can go up to 2 minutes.

## Hook (first 3 seconds)
- Open on a **question or a sharp opinion** in your voice from word one, with the headline on screen at the same time (`styles/storytelling.md`: stack the audio, visual and text hooks).
- Visual hook: the most striking image in the piece, or a full-screen typographic **headline card** (see Graphics).
- Text hook: the title as an editorial headline, e.g. "THE ART OF / SLOW LIVING" or "Not every hobby / needs to be *monetized.*" (moodboard: The Art of Slow Living spread, Longform cover)
- Cuts in the first 3 seconds: 1-2. Calmer than gym or history, but never static for the first 2 seconds.

## Pacing
- Average shot length: 2.5-4 seconds. Let images breathe more than in the history style. Hold up to 6 seconds on a stat, a quote card or a meaningful diary moment.
- Pauses kept between sentences: about 0.3s. Keep the conversational rhythm and a laugh or aside if it's good. Still cut "um"s and retakes.
- Cut timing: on voiceover phrases, one new image per idea
- Speed changes: none. Slow push-ins on photos are fine (3-5% over the shot).
- Structure each piece like a magazine article, with chapter cards between sections: "01 / The problem", "02 / What I found", "03 / What I think".

## Captions and on-screen text
- **Spoken captions**: clean sans or a book serif in sentence case (not all caps), warm cream text (#F2EBDD) on dark footage or black (#111111) on cream backgrounds. About 54px, 3-6 words at a time, centered at about 72% from the top. The key word goes in *italic* or burgundy (#7A1F2B). No boxes and no bouncing animation, just a simple cut or a 4-frame fade.
- **Headlines**: a bold, high-contrast display serif in heavy or italic weight, such as Playfair Display Black or DM Serif Display, 120-180px. Use tight line spacing and break the headline across 2-3 lines (moodboard: The Rambler, Longform, RANE). Use one word at huge size for emphasis (moodboard: "SO / LO", RANE).
- **Kickers and labels**: small spaced capitals in a monospace or sans, about 28px, letter-spacing +15%, e.g. "ISSUE 04 · THE CITY", "A QUIET INTERLUDE", "STREET / PORTRAIT / CITY" (moodboard: Longform, Plumwood, Paris).
- **Pull quotes**: a serif italic between large quotation marks, centered on cream, for the one line you want remembered (moodboard: Slow Living spread)
- **Stats**: one big number in the display serif, with a one-line source in small caps underneath. Always name the source on screen.
- Emojis: never

## Footage and framing
- **Film-photo look**: grain, a slight halation glow on lights, and night streets under green fluorescent or warm sodium light. Use flash photos, shop windows and train platforms (moodboard: Trust the Process collage, Paris street photography).
- **Collage and grid layouts**: 2-6 photos on screen at once in a magazine-style grid with thin gutters, for "here are many examples" moments (moodboard: Trust the Process, Sociotype portrait grid)
- **Video-essay frames**: long, still, observational shots of everyday places (corridors, streets, people from behind), paired with something personal such as a handwritten letter or notebook page (moodboard: the Chantal Akerman video essay)
- **Visual diary**: POV shots of your hands, coffee, walks and notebook. Shoot on a tripod or rest the phone on something. Never film yourself facing the camera.
- **Mini-documentary**: archival photos and footage, maps and simple data charts. Recolor charts in the brand palette.
- Zooms / punch-ins: slow push-ins only

## Graphics
- Make every text card look like a **magazine page**: a cream or forest-green background, a thin rule line, the kicker at the top, the headline, and an optional subtitle in serif italic (moodboard: Plumwood Mountain, Slow Living, Longform).
- Recurring elements give the brand its look, so reuse them in every video: a small series label in the top corner (e.g. "No. 07"), numbered chapters ("01 /") and the same end card.
- Data visualizations: flat and simple, using the brand colors only, with a cream background and black or burgundy bars.
- Tool to use: HyperFrames for headline cards, chapter cards, quote cards and collages. Remotion for word-timed captions and charts.

## Transitions
- Mostly hard cuts. Use a **page-turn or slide** from a footage section into a text card, and a 6-frame dip to black between chapters.

## Color
- **Brand palette**:
  - Black #111111 (backgrounds, text)
  - Warm cream #F2EBDD (backgrounds, captions on dark)
  - Deep burgundy #7A1F2B (highlight words, rules, one accent per screen)
  - Forest green #2F4A3A (section cards, chart fills)
- Footage grade: warm, with slightly lifted blacks and muted saturation (-10%). Keep greens deep and reds rich so they echo the palette. Add light film grain to everything, footage and cards included, so it all feels like one publication.
- Black-and-white is allowed for archival footage and for moments that are emotional or in the past.

## Music & sound effects
- Music type: understated instrumentals such as piano, soft jazz, lo-fi or ambient strings. Never "motivational" epic music.
- Music level: about 20 dB under the voice. Your voice always leads.
- Sound effects: subtle and tactile only, such as a page turn on text cards, a camera shutter on photos or a pen scratch on handwriting
- Final loudness: -14 LUFS

## Ending
- End on **your takeaway**, one sentence that is your perspective, shown as a pull-quote card
- Then a 1.5s end card in the same layout every time: the series name or page name as a headline and "No. __" as a kicker. Don't use "like and subscribe". At most add a soft line such as "more essays like this on the page".

## Do / Don't
*Your corrections, saved as rules. Never deleted, only added to or refined.*

**Do**
- Always use your own voice and keep your personality (asides, humor, your accent)
- Show sources on screen for every statistic or claim
- Reuse the same fonts, colors and end card in every video, so it's recognizable on sight

**Don't**
- Don't show your face (for now)
- Don't use AI-generated narration
- Don't make it look like generic motivational content: no stock "hustle" clips, no neon text, no epic music
- Don't copy the moodboard designs exactly. Use their ideas (layout, type, color) in your own design.

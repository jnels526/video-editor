# Storytelling rules (all styles)

> Applies to **every** video, together with the style file for that video (history, gym or travel).
> Where a style file gives a more specific rule, the style file wins.
> Source: "Ultimate STORYTELLING masterclass", Jade Beason (YouTube, 11:45), summarized on 2026-11-27. These are ideas written in our own words, not her script.

## 1. Every video is a tiny story
Every video has three parts. Before cutting, write them down in one line each in the cut list.
- **Character**: who we're watching, and why the viewer should care (you, a historical figure, the place)
- **Conflict / tension**: the problem, the question or the "will she make it?" moment (ref @ 1:00)
- **Resolution**: the payoff, such as the answer, the completed rep or the reveal (ref @ 1:30)
Even a talking-head or tip video follows **problem, then process, then outcome** (ref @ 2:00).

## 2. Hook in the first 1-2 seconds
- **Start at the emotional peak**, then fill in context afterwards (ref @ 6:00). If the clip has no clear peak, use a strong question or claim instead.
- **Stack the hooks**: put a *visual* hook (something happens on screen in frame 1) together with a *text* hook (scroll-stopping words), and an *audio* hook too if there's speech (the first sentence speaks to the exact viewer) (ref @ 7:00-8:00).
- **ABT formula** for a one-line hook (ref @ 8:30): **And** (who it's for and the situation), **But** (the problem), **Therefore** (what they'll get).
  Example: "you train hard *and* eat well, *but* your hips are always tight, *so* try this."

## 3. Keep them watching
- **Story loops**: tease something early and pay it off later ("wait for the last set", "the price surprised me"). Close every loop you open (ref @ 10:00).
- **Visual shortcuts in the edit**: use on-screen text to underline plot points, and B-roll or reaction shots to show what's being said (ref @ 3:00).
- **Match B-roll and music to the emotion** of the moment: tense, funny or triumphant (ref @ 4:30).
- When there's speech, use **word-by-word captions** (ref @ 8:00).

## 4. Endings
- End on the resolution. A soft call to action is fine ("worth the trip", "save this for leg day"). Don't hard-sell (ref @ 11:00).

## How this applies to each style
| | Character | Tension | Payoff | Hook | Loop idea |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **history** | The forgotten person | The injustice or mystery | What really happened | A surprising claim plus an archival photo in frame 1 | "...and that's not even the strangest part" |
| **gym** | You | The heavy weight, or the problem in the promise line | The completed rep or the fix | The hardest rep in frame 1 plus the promise text (ABT) | "watch the last rep" |
| **travel** | You (faceless) or the place | "Is it worth it?" or a meme setup | The reveal or the verdict | The best view in frame 1 plus the setup text | A teased price or view that pays off at the end |
